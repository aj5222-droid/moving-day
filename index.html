    Cost of each add

    The moves add up to less than 2n

    Doubling means the moves cost 1, 2, 4, 8 and so on. Drag the slider and watch the pieces stack up. They always fit inside a bar of length 2n.

    Each move copies a whole building, and each building is twice the last. Any sum like 1 + 2 + 4 + ... + m is smaller than 2m, and the last move is smaller than n. So all the moves together copy fewer than 2n boxes. Add the n boxes you placed and the total is under 3n. That is about $3 per add, however many adds you do. This is what amortized O(1) means.

    A flat fee that never runs out

    Charge the customer a fixed fee for every add and keep the extra in a jar. The jar pays for the moves. Can you pick a fee that never lets the jar go negative?

    What real languages pick

    Every constant factor above 1 is amortized O(1). The factor only decides the constant: a small factor wastes little memory but moves often, a big one moves rarely but wastes space.

Real vectors can also shrink, which needs its own rule (shrink at a quarter full, not half, or alternating add and remove would move forever).

    Moving Day

    An array is a building. Each slot is a room. Adding a box is easy until the building is full.

    Then you have to move every box into a bigger building, and each move costs money. So how big should the next building be?
